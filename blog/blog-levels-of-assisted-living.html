What Are the Levels of Assisted Living?

2026-10-24

Senior care isn't one-size-fits-all. Families searching for the right setting often hear terms like "independent living," "assisted living," "memory care," and "skilled nursing" without a clear sense of how they differ. Here's a straightforward look at each level, and where a congregate care home like Renewity fits in.

Independent Living

This level is for seniors who don't need daily help but want the convenience of a community setting — think meals, housekeeping, and social activities without the upkeep of a private home. Independent living doesn't typically include hands-on personal care.

Assisted Living

Assisted living adds support with daily tasks like bathing, dressing, medication reminders, and mobility. Residents still maintain a fair amount of independence, but staff are available to help when needed. This is often the right fit for seniors who are safe living semi-independently but could use a helping hand throughout the day.

Congregate Care

Congregate care, like what Renewity offers, sits close to assisted living but in a smaller, more residential setting. Instead of a large facility, residents live in a home-like environment with closer staff attention and a smaller group of neighbors. Renewity is a congregate care home in New Albany, Indiana — and importantly, it is not a skilled nursing facility, so it's best suited for seniors who need support with daily living rather than ongoing medical treatment.

Memory Care

Memory care is designed specifically for seniors living with Alzheimer's or other forms of dementia. It typically involves a more structured environment and staff trained to support memory-related needs. If you're exploring this option, you can learn more on our care page.

Skilled Nursing

Skilled nursing facilities provide the highest level of medical care, including services like wound care, IV therapy, and rehabilitation after surgery or illness. This level is typically reserved for seniors with significant medical needs that go beyond daily living assistance.

Finding the Right Fit

Choosing the right level of care comes down to understanding what your loved one needs today — and being honest about what they may need down the road.
