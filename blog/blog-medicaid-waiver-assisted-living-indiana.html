Does a Medicaid Waiver Pay for Assisted Living in Indiana?

2026-11-21

Sometimes — but it depends on the program, the facility, and your loved one's specific eligibility. Indiana does offer Medicaid waiver programs designed to help cover certain long-term care costs outside of a nursing home, but the details can be confusing. Here's a plain-English overview to help you start asking the right questions.

What Is a Medicaid Waiver, Generally?

A Medicaid waiver is a state-administered program that allows some Medicaid funds, which traditionally only cover nursing home care, to instead be used for home- and community-based services. The idea is to give seniors options besides a skilled nursing facility.

Not every assisted living or residential care setting participates in these waiver programs, and not every senior qualifies. Eligibility usually depends on income, assets, and level of care needed — details that can change, so it's worth confirming current rules directly with the state or a knowledgeable care provider.

Why This Gets Confusing for Families

Congregate care homes, assisted living communities, and skilled nursing facilities are all different types of care settings, and payment rules don't always look the same across them. A waiver that applies to one type of setting may not apply to another.

This is one reason it helps to ask very specific questions early: Does this particular home accept Medicaid waiver funding? What level of care qualifies? What paperwork or waiting period is involved? The answers can vary quite a bit from one provider to the next.

Renewity Is a Congregate Care Home — Not a Skilled Nursing Facility

It's worth knowing that Renewity operates as a congregate care home rather than a skilled nursing facility. That distinction matters when it comes to how payment programs work, since Medicaid waiver rules are often tied to the specific type of care setting.

If you're trying to understand how a congregate care home like ours differs from assisted living or nursing care, our post on facility versus home-style care can help give you some helpful context before you dig into payment questions.

What Families Can Do Next

  • Ask directly whether a specific waiver applies to the type of care setting you're considering.
  • Have your loved one's current care needs and financial information ready — eligibility is usually tied to both.
  • Don't assume one provider's answer applies everywhere; policies and participation can differ home to home.

Because waiver rules and eligibility can be detailed and specific to each family's situation, we always encourage families to ask us directly about how it applies at Renewity, rather than relying on general information alone.
